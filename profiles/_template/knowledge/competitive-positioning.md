# Competitive positioning

Replace this file with your own. It is what `battlecard` reads; a competitor with no entry here
gets a battlecard that says so, rather than one assembled from recollection.

## The rule that governs every entry

**Never trash their solution.** A seller who runs a competitor down tells the buyer two things:
that the buyer has poor judgement for shortlisting them, and that we would talk about *them* this
way to the next account. State where we fit, where they fit, and let the buyer decide — the
complementary framing wins more often than the adversarial one, and it survives the call where the
competitor is already installed.

The corollary: **write down where they genuinely win.** An entry with no honest "where they win"
row is not a battlecard, it is a brochure, and the first buyer who has used both will know it.

## Per competitor

### `<competitor name>`

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **What they are** | one sentence, as *they* would describe themselves — not as we would |
| **Where they genuinely win** | the cases where the honest recommendation is them |
| **Where we win** | the cases where we are the better answer, with the reason, not the adjective |
| **Overlap** | what both do, where a buyer reasonably sees a choice |
| **Complement** | where we sit alongside rather than instead — often the most useful row |
| **Their pricing shape** | model, not a number, unless the number is public and dated |
| **Last verified** | a date, and the source |

## Facts with a shelf life

Anything about a competitor's product is true on a date. Record the date with the claim, and treat
an undated competitive fact as unverified — a feature gap closed six months ago and quoted on a
call is the fastest way to lose the room.
